# Rumuze Sprint 3: Positioning and Conversion Architecture

Date: 2026-04-08  
Owner: Positioning, Conversion, Product Marketing  
Status: Sprint 3 definition

## 1. Objective

Sprint 3 converts Rumuze from a technically credible company into a commercially legible one.

The goal is not to sound bigger. The goal is to make high-intent buyers understand:

1. who Rumuze is for
2. what problem Rumuze solves
3. what they can buy
4. what happens next after they click

This document defines the positioning, offer architecture, homepage conversion structure, CTA system, and intake logic for that outcome.

---

## 2. Final Positioning

### 2.1 Core Positioning Statement

Rumuze builds the operational systems and revenue infrastructure for B2B companies that have outgrown fragmented vendors, under-scoped software projects, and marketing that cannot be tied to pipeline.

### 2.2 What This Means in Plain Terms

Rumuze is not a generic agency, and not a commodity software shop.

Rumuze is a structured delivery partner for companies that need:

- a serious web or SaaS platform
- connected CRM, attribution, and reporting infrastructure
- bilingual Arabic-English execution when the market requires it
- one accountable team instead of separate software, marketing, and analytics vendors

### 2.3 What Rumuze Is Not

Rumuze is not for:

- low-budget brochure websites
- companies shopping for the cheapest dev vendor
- generic social media management retainers
- early-stage teams with no defined commercial process
- clients who want output volume without governance or accountability

## 4. Core Problem

## 4.1 Specific Problem Statement

The companies Rumuze should target are not failing because they lack activity.

They are failing because their commercial system is fragmented:

- the website does not support the sales process
- CRM usage is partial or inconsistent
- marketing is producing traffic or leads without reliable attribution
- software work is scoped as isolated features instead of business infrastructure
- executive reporting cannot connect spend, system behavior, and pipeline quality

## 4.2 Why This Problem Is Painful

This creates measurable damage:

- lead leakage before sales follow-up
- wasted paid spend due to broken tracking and weak qualification
- slow internal execution because systems are not integrated
- repeated rebuilds because the first implementation was under-scoped
- no confident answer to: what is generating qualified pipeline, what is not, and where the system is breaking

## 4.3 The Measurable Version

The pain should be framed around operational and commercial failure, not vanity:

- leads are coming in but not being routed correctly
- sales cannot trust source data
- campaigns are running but attribution is weak
- the website exists but does not qualify or convert the right buyers
- internal teams are patching around system gaps with manual work

- software engineering capability

## 7. Offer Architecture

Rumuze should sell 3 core offers, not a menu of generic services.

Each offer must feel like a buying decision, not a vague collaboration.

## 7.1 Offer 1: System Build

### Working Name

System Build

### Who It Is For

- companies replacing an underperforming website or portal
- companies moving from fragmented tools to a unified platform
- companies launching a serious B2B web platform, SaaS module, or operational system tied to sales or delivery

### Problem It Solves

The buyer has a business process that matters commercially, but the current system is broken, disconnected, or too weak to support growth.

### Tangible Deliverables

- scoped solution architecture
- delivery roadmap with phases and milestones
- UX / page or workflow map
- application build or rebuild
- CRM integration plan and implementation
- analytics and conversion event architecture
- handoff documentation
- launch plan and acceptance criteria

### Expected Outcome

- a production-ready system that supports qualification, routing, reporting, and revenue operations
- fewer manual handoffs
- stronger control over how leads and customer actions move through the business

### Pricing Logic

Model:

- fixed-fee, scoped delivery by phase

Price driver inputs:

- number of workflows/modules
- integration complexity
- bilingual scope
- reporting and analytics depth
- governance and rollout complexity

Commercial rule:

- sold as a defined build with milestones, not day-rate execution

## 7.2 Offer 2: System Audit

### Working Name

System Audit

### Who It Is For

- buyers who know something is broken but cannot isolate the failure
- teams preparing for a rebuild, regional expansion, or vendor transition
- companies with traffic, leads, or software already in market but low confidence in system performance

### Problem It Solves

The buyer lacks diagnostic clarity. They do not know whether the problem is platform, funnel, CRM, tracking, attribution, routing, or all of the above.

### Tangible Deliverables

- current-state system map
- commercial bottleneck analysis
- website / platform audit
- CRM and lead routing audit
- analytics and attribution audit
- severity-ranked findings
- priority action plan
- decision memo: repair, rebuild, or re-architect

### Expected Outcome

- leadership gains a clear picture of where revenue is leaking
- internal teams get a prioritized action plan instead of opinions
- buyer can decide whether to fix internally, assign to current vendors, or move into a Rumuze implementation

### Pricing Logic

Model:

- fixed-fee diagnostic engagement

Price driver inputs:

- number of systems under review
- number of traffic / funnel sources
- reporting and CRM complexity
- stakeholder interview count

Commercial rule:

- audit should be standalone and valuable on its own
- audit fee can optionally credit toward a larger build if approved within a defined window

## 7.3 Offer 3: Growth Infrastructure Setup

### Working Name

Growth Infrastructure Setup

### Who It Is For

- companies already running sales and campaigns but with weak tracking, attribution, and reporting
- businesses with a functioning site or app but no reliable revenue measurement layer
- commercial teams preparing to scale ad spend or improve funnel efficiency

### Problem It Solves

Marketing activity exists, but the company cannot trust what is driving qualified pipeline.

### Tangible Deliverables

- event and conversion taxonomy
- tracking architecture
- server-side tracking setup where relevant
- CRM source mapping
- lead stage and lifecycle definitions
- attribution model setup
- executive reporting dashboard
- documented KPI definitions and reporting cadence

### Expected Outcome

- buyer can measure which channels and campaigns produce qualified pipeline
- sales, growth, and leadership operate from one reporting logic
- spend decisions become more defensible

### Pricing Logic

Model:

- fixed-fee implementation engagement
- optional monthly optimization extension after setup

Price driver inputs:

- number of channels
- CRM maturity
- tracking stack complexity
- dashboard/reporting requirements
- number of lifecycle stages and automation rules

Commercial rule:

- sell setup as infrastructure implementation first
- optimization retainer comes only after the measurement layer is live

We build the systems B2B companies need to scale revenue without losing operational control.

Rumuze designs and delivers web platforms, CRM-connected workflows, tracking architecture, and attribution infrastructure for B2B companies in the Gulf and wider MENA region that have outgrown fragmented vendors and disconnected systems.

Make the buyer recognize the operational and revenue cost of the current setup.

Most growth problems are system problems.

Your company does not need more disconnected activity. It needs a commercial system that works end to end.

### Broken-state bullets

- the website attracts attention but does not support qualification or routing
- CRM data is inconsistent, partial, or ignored by the commercial team
- paid and organic channels generate activity, but attribution is not trusted
- leadership cannot see where pipeline quality is improving or degrading
- internal teams are compensating with spreadsheets, manual follow-up, and vendor coordination

### Why it matters

This is not a branding problem. It is a revenue efficiency problem.

## 8.3 Section 3: Solution

### Goal

Explain what Rumuze actually builds.

### Section headline

Rumuze builds the commercial operating layer.

### Section copy

We design and implement the platform, CRM-connected workflows, tracking architecture, and reporting logic that let growth, sales, and operations work from the same system.

### Three solution pillars

1. Platform Systems
   - web platforms
   - SaaS modules
   - portals
   - operational workflows

2. Revenue Infrastructure
   - CRM integration
   - tracking architecture
   - attribution logic
   - reporting dashboards

3. Delivery Governance
   - scoped milestones
   - documented outputs
   - implementation accountability
   - launch readiness

## 8.4 Section 4: Proof

### Goal

Create confidence without overclaiming.

### Required structure

Use placeholders initially, but keep the structure fixed:

1. Case study card
   - client type
   - problem
   - what was built
   - measured outcome
   - proof label:
     - verified
     - internal benchmark
     - illustrative

2. Delivery proof card
   - engagement type
   - duration range
   - outputs delivered

3. Commercial proof card
   - pipeline, routing, reporting, or operational improvement
   - source status

### Rules

- no anonymous giant claims without context
- no “we prove results” wording without evidence
- no aggregate vanity metrics unless claim registry status is confirmed

## 9. CTA System

## 9.1 Primary CTA

### Label

Book a Systems Discovery

### Buyer intent

High intent. Buyer knows there is a meaningful systems or revenue infrastructure problem and is open to a scoped engagement.

### Placement

- hero
- sticky nav CTA
- mid-page after problem/solution
- final CTA block

### Post-click flow

1. open structured intake form
2. buyer selects engagement type
3. buyer submits qualification details
4. buyer sees next-step confirmation
5. internal team reviews within one business day

## 9.2 Secondary CTA

### Label

Request a System Audit

### Buyer intent

Medium-to-high intent. Buyer knows something is broken but is not ready to commit to a build.

### Placement

- hero secondary CTA
- offer card for audit
- proof section
- final CTA block

### Post-click flow

1. open audit-specific intake
2. collect system, funnel, and reporting context
3. route to audit triage
4. confirm whether audit is the right entry point

## 9.3 Offer-Specific CTA Labels

- System Build: `Book a Build Consultation`
- System Audit: `Request a System Audit`
- Growth Infrastructure Setup: `Start Infrastructure Setup`

## 9.4 CTA Rules

1. Do not use `Contact Us` as the main CTA.
2. Every CTA must imply a concrete commercial next step.
3. CTA labels must match an actual offer or intake path.
4. Hero CTA and final CTA should lead to the same primary path for consistency.

## 10. Conversion Logic

## 10.1 User Journey

### Path A: High-intent buyer

Landing  
-> reads hero  
-> identifies problem in section 2  
-> sees relevant offer  
-> clicks `Book a Systems Discovery`  
-> submits structured intake  
-> receives confirmation and next-step timeline  
-> internal qualification  
-> discovery call or disqualification

### Path B: Diagnostic buyer

Landing  
-> recognizes fragmentation problem  
-> clicks `Request a System Audit`  
-> submits audit intake  
-> internal review  
-> audit proposal or redirect to build consultation

### Path C: Lower-friction commercial curiosity

Landing  
-> explores proof and offers  
-> clicks secondary CTA  
-> enters intake  
-> routed to qualification with lower urgency expectations

## 10.3 Lead Qualification Logic

Each lead should be scored on four axes:

1. Commercial fit
   - B2B / serious service model
   - non-trivial deal value
   - operational need

2. Problem fit
   - clear system fragmentation
   - clear attribution/reporting gap
   - rebuild or integration need

3. Budget fit
   - buyer understands this is a scoped high-ticket engagement

4. Urgency and ownership
   - there is an internal decision-maker
   - there is a live business reason to act

### Routing outcomes

- High fit:
  - book discovery with a named lead
- Medium fit:
  - recommend System Audit
- Low fit:
  - polite decline or redirect to lighter-weight path

## 10.4 What Happens After Submission

### Immediate confirmation screen

Message:

Your request has been received. We will review fit, clarify scope if needed, and respond within one business day with the recommended next step.

### Internal workflow

1. assign lead owner
2. review ICP fit
3. classify into:
   - discovery
   - audit
   - infrastructure setup
   - not a fit
4. send response with one defined next action

### Response SLA

- one business day for first response
- three business days maximum for scope recommendation

## 12. Key Decisions Made

1. Rumuze will position around commercial systems, not broad creativity or general software execution.
2. The ICP is narrowed to B2B companies in GCC/MENA with meaningful operational and revenue complexity.
3. The homepage will sell 3 offers, not a long services menu.
4. The primary CTA will be a systems discovery, not generic contact.
5. The audit offer will serve as the lower-risk entry point for uncertain buyers.
6. Lead qualification will prioritize fit, urgency, and operational need over raw inquiry volume.
7. Messaging will center on fragmented systems, weak attribution, and missing accountability because those are the real pains Rumuze can credibly address.
